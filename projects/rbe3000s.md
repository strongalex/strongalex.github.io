<h1>4 DOF Inverse Kinematics, ROS 2, and Autonomous Navigation</h1>

<p>The RBE 3000-level sequence took me from the math of a single robot arm all the way to a mobile robot that could map an unknown space and plan its own route through it. The work split into three parts.</p>

<h2>Manipulator Kinematics</h2>
<p>I derived and implemented full forward and inverse kinematics for a 4-DOF manipulator (the OpenManipulator-X) using DH parameterization, along with a velocity Jacobian for the arm. Everything was written in MATLAB and validated against a custom interactive 3D model, so I could drag the end effector around and confirm the joint solutions matched the hardware before ever commanding the real arm.</p>

<h2>Vision-Guided Pick and Place</h2>
<p>On top of the kinematics I built a computer vision pipeline that let the arm sort colored spheres into bins with no human input:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Intrinsic and extrinsic camera calibration to tie pixel coordinates to the robot's frame</li>
  <li>HSV color segmentation and centroid detection to locate each ball</li>
  <li>A geometric height-correction model to account for the fact that a sphere's apparent centroid shifts with its distance from the camera</li>
</ul>

<h2>Autonomous Navigation</h2>
<p>The last portion moved to a TurtleBot3 running ROS 2. I designed a layered software architecture that combined a Behavior Tree for high-level mission control with a Finite State Machine for navigation, plus a request-reply Dispatcher node that handed out multi-stop delivery tasks and triggered replanning when an obstacle appeared. Underneath that I implemented:</p>
<ul>
  <li>A* path planning with configuration-space obstacle inflation and risk-weighted costing</li>
  <li>Frontier-based SLAM exploration to map an unknown environment</li>
  <li>Particle-filter re-localization to recover the robot's pose within a known map</li>
</ul>
