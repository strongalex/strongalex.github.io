<h1>Honolulu Bike Share Network Optimization</h1>
<p><em>Interactive Qualifying Project &mdash; Secure Bike Share, Honolulu, HI &mdash; Aug. to Dec. 2025</em></p>

<p>My team spent a term in Honolulu working with Secure Bike Share, the operator of the city's bike share system, on where the network should grow and why people were not riding it more.</p>

<h2>Where the Stations Should Go</h2>
<p>I built suitability models in ArcGIS Pro that layered census demographics, transit access, and existing bike-lane coverage to identify candidate sites for new stations. I paired that with a ridership-weighted ranking of the existing stations, which gave the operator a defensible shortlist of underperforming stations worth relocating rather than a gut-feel guess.</p>

<h2>Asking the Riders</h2>
<p>We designed a demographically conditioned survey and distributed it through the operator's 20,000-person mailing list under IRB approval. Analyzing the 233 responses isolated two dominant barriers to ridership: the quality of the surrounding bike infrastructure and the supply of available bikes &mdash; not pricing or app experience, which the client had assumed were the bigger problems.</p>

<h2>Deliverables</h2>
<p>I authored the team's final technical report and the client-facing improvement plan. That plan included a matched control-group methodology for measuring whether QR way-finding signage actually increases bus-to-bike transfers, so the operator could test the intervention rather than just deploy it.</p>
